Task focus: what exactly are you asked to describe or outline?

Many comprehension tasks are selective: you must leave out information that does not answer the question. Comprehension is not yet an analysis of how stage directions, language or interactions create meaning.

Common operators

outline · main pointsdescribe · relevant detailssummarise / sum up · concise accountstate · briefly present

How to approach the task

Select & organise

Underline the focus words in the task. Identify relevant ideas, attitudes, relationships or events. Group related information logically instead of following every line of the scene.

Write clearly

Paraphrase in your own words, use the simple present and maintain a neutral tone. Begin with a brief text reference if appropriate; avoid interpretation of techniques and an unnecessary concluding opinion.

Explain how a dramatic scene is presented and develops, what it reveals about the characters and what the dramatic techniques contribute to its meaning.

Exam focus

“Focus on interaction, stage directions and use of language.” All three must be addressed when specified by the task; they do not have to receive equal weight or appear in separate paragraphs. Select meaningful evidence and connect the techniques to one coherent interpretation.

Understand the interaction

What do the characters want, believe or expect? What do their responses reveal about their relationships, overt disagreements or underlying tensions?

Trace the development

Notice how tone, tension, attitudes and communication shift. Use opening · development · critical moment · outcome flexibly, never as a fixed four-step formula.

Explore the dramatic presentation

Analyse speaking turns and responses, non-verbal behaviour and stage directions, as well as syntax, tone, word choice and selected devices.

Write a line of analysis

Form a hypothesis and organise paragraphs around meaningful aspects. Support each claim with precise evidence and explain function, meaning and significance.

Read the task carefully: an argumentative comment and a creative re-creation require different forms of writing.

A · Comment / Evaluation

Develop a position

Identify the issue, construct a clear argument and support it with examples and knowledge gained in class. Where appropriate, consider a different perspective before reaching a reasoned conclusion.

Use context deliberately

Connect ideas from the drama to relevant historical, cultural or social contexts, if the task requires it. Do not retell the scene instead of discussing the issue.

Argumentative writing ↗Structure and language for a comment, discussion or evaluation.

B · Re-creation of Text

For a continuation or rewriting of a scene, remain faithful to the characterisation, relationships and situation established by the text while creating your own convincing dramatic interaction.

From analysing drama to writing drama

Plan a credible progression: opening → development → critical moment → outcome. This model supports your writing; it does not require a quarrel, a dramatic climax or complete resolution. A realisation, refusal, change of attitude or tentative understanding can also move a scene forward.

Interaction & development

Let each line respond meaningfully to what has been said or done. Show how expectations, tensions or understanding change.

Language & performance

Use character-appropriate words, tone and sentence patterns. Add stage directions where gestures, pauses or movements contribute to meaning. Do not force dialect or exaggerated stereotypes.

Remember: A creative text should develop a dramatic situation, not simply explain the characters' feelings in dialogue.

Mediate relevant information from a German source for an English-speaking addressee in a specific communicative situation. You are not producing a word-for-word translation.

Read the task before the source text

Determine the context, addressee, purpose, intended text type and register. These determine what you include, how you organise it and whether cultural references need explanation.

Six strategies for successful mediation

Select information

Choose only what the addressee needs. Focus on the task and leave out irrelevant examples and details.

Restructure information

Do not automatically copy the source order. Group related ideas and create a coherent message for the new situation.

Summarise information

Condense details appropriately without changing the meaning. Preserve essential distinctions, figures and qualifications.

Paraphrase naturally

Reformulate concepts in idiomatic English instead of translating every German word. Explain unfamiliar terms rather than copying them.

Report objectively

Represent the source accurately, distinguish its claims from your own views and avoid adding unsupported information.

Bridge cultural gaps

Consider what readers may not know. Explain relevant German institutions, practices or cultural references briefly and respectfully.

Form matters: write for the addressee

Email

Use an appropriate greeting, reason for writing, logically organised information and suitable closing. Match the relationship and degree of formality.

Informative article / post

Address the intended audience, provide an informative opening, use clear paragraphs and keep the register suitable for publication.

Before handing in

Have you answered the specific question, selected and reorganised the relevant content, paraphrased independently, preserved the original meaning, provided necessary cultural explanations and used the required text type?

Final exam check

  • I have followed the exact task focus and operator.
  • I have selected relevant information or evidence.
  • I use the appropriate text type and register.
  • My text is clearly structured and linked.
  • I make meanings explicit rather than merely describing or listing.
  • I have checked accuracy, language and task completion.
