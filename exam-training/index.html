Dramatic dialogue analysis

From understanding to interpretation

Use the following questions to select and connect the most significant observations from the scene.

Understanding the interactionWhat do the characters want, expect or fear? What does their behaviour reveal about relationships, open disagreements, underlying tensions or subtext? Not every dialogue contains an obvious conflict.
Tracing dramatic developmentWhat changes? Look for shifts in tone, power, attitudes or understanding. Use opening, development, critical moment, outcome as a flexible model, not a fixed formula.
Interaction & communicationExamine linked speaking turns, questions, interruptions, silences and patterns of reacting, challenging, avoiding or cooperating.
Stage directions & performanceAnalyse gestures, movement, positioning, pauses and props. How do these actions support or contradict what the characters say?
Use of languageConsider syntax, tone, word choice, register and relevant stylistic devices. Explain what these choices mean in this interaction.
Writing a line of analysisDevelop an interpretative hypothesis, organise connected analytical paragraphs, integrate precise textual evidence and explain its significance.

Exam focus: If the task specifies interaction, stage directions and use of language, address all three. You do not have to give them equal attention or write three separate sections. Choose meaningful evidence and explain how the techniques work together.

Planning your scene

Developing a dramatic interaction

Use the four-phase model as a planning aid, not a formula that every dialogue must follow.

OpeningEstablish the situation and what the characters want.
DevelopmentShow how statements, reactions and expectations move the exchange forward.
Critical momentBuild towards a meaningful response, decision, realisation or shift — not necessarily an argument.
OutcomeShow what has changed or remains unresolved; an open ending may be appropriate.

Key strategies

Six strategies for effective mediation

Use the six-step approach from our Mediation handout. Select what is relevant to the task.

1 · Selecting informationChoose the information that answers the task and that the addressee needs. Leave out irrelevant details.
2 · Restructuring informationOrganise the content logically for the new situation. The source order does not need to be retained.
3 · Summarising informationCondense details while preserving essential facts, distinctions and qualifications.
4 · Paraphrasing / languageUse your own words and natural English rather than translating literally. Explain unfamiliar terms if necessary.
5 · Reporting objectivelyRepresent the source accurately and neutrally. Do not add unsupported facts or personal opinions.
6 · Coping with cultureConsider what your reader may not know. Explain relevant institutions, customs and cultural concepts briefly and respectfully.

Before handing in: Check that your message matches the required text type (e.g. email or informative article), addresses its reader appropriately, conveys the relevant content and explains cultural references where necessary.
